Atmospheric science

Zhenyu You

Organized convection.
Large-scale circulation.
Precipitation extremes.

I study how organized convection interacts with large-scale circulation to shape regional precipitation and extreme weather. My research combines observations, climate model evaluation, and idealized dynamical models to develop a physical understanding of these interactions.

I am a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at Boston College, working with Yi Ming in the Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences and the Schiller Institute for Integrated Science and Society.

01

Research

Organized convection across scales

I examine how large-scale circulation and environmental wind profiles influence the development and propagation of mesoscale convective systems, from springtime U.S. storms to diurnal rainfall over the Maritime Continent.

Circulation and precipitation extremes

Using idealized dynamical models, I study how convective heating feeds back on synoptic variability and how evolving planetary-scale circulation shapes regional precipitation extremes.

Physical understanding of model biases

I diagnose the physical mechanisms behind errors in simulated convection and precipitation, connecting regional storm behavior to the dynamical background in high-resolution models.
